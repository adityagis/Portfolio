01

Overview

The Kosi is called the sorrow of Bihar. It floods almost every monsoon and has shifted its course many times. This map covers the Kosi region of north Bihar: Darbhanga, Madhubani, Saharsa, Supaul, Madhepura and Forbesganj. I went to school in Darbhanga, so this is home ground for me.

Move the slider and the map shades all land at or below that elevation. The land here is very flat. It drops from about 75 m near the Nepal border to about 40 m in the south, so a few metres of water covers a very large area. The map shows this in a way a table of numbers cannot.

The map works fully in the browser. It reads public elevation tiles directly, decodes the height of every pixel, and colours it on the fly. There is no server and no preprocessing.

02

Tech stack

  • Leaflet
  • JavaScript (Canvas)
  • Terrain Tiles (SRTM-based DEM)
  • OpenStreetMap basemap
  • GitHub Pages

03

Process

Problem → Data → Method → System → Result

  1. 01 Problem

    Show people in the Kosi region, in one picture, how much of their land lies low.

  2. 02 Data

    Public Terrarium elevation tiles (Terrain Tiles on AWS, built mainly from SRTM) and an OpenStreetMap basemap.

  3. 03 Method

    Each tile's colour values are decoded to height in metres (R×256 + G + B/256 − 32768). Pixels at or below the chosen elevation are shaded, darker for lower ground.

  4. 04 System

    A Leaflet web map with a custom canvas layer, an elevation slider, a live pointer readout, and the share of land in view below the chosen level.

  5. 05 Result

    A fast interactive map that runs on any phone or laptop, with no server.

04

Results and limits

The map makes the scale of low-lying land in the Kosi region easy to see. At 50 m, most of Saharsa and Madhepura is shaded, while Forbesganj and Madhubani stay mostly dry.

This is an elevation view, not a flood forecast. Real flooding also depends on embankments, river channels, rainfall and drainage. The DEM has a resolution of about 30 m and can be off by a few metres. A full flood-risk model would add these layers, as I did in my flood risk mapping work at DRDO.
